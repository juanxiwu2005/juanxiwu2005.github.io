American Airlines: hardware roadmap & device refresh standard

Technology Procurement Intern · June–August 2026 · returning full-time 2027
Landed
Problem

Each business unit planned device refreshes on its own timeline. IT, Finance and the business disagreed on how long devices should last, so procurement saw demand late and bought in small batches.

Approach
  • Combined historical actuals, asset data, contracts and 60+ business cases into one multi-year view
  • Modeled consolidated demand for 69K+ iPads and rugged devices to turn scattered refresh plans into volume-based sourcing
  • Gathered requirements from 20+ stakeholders across IT, Finance and business units, plus Gartner advisory input
Output

A 2026–2031 hardware roadmap across seven device categories, a procurement-led refresh standard, and a lifecycle-governance proposal with an M365 Copilot concept for monitoring hardware market signals. Presented to the Chief Procurement Officer.

PHX

Deloitte-sponsored case: post-merger procurement synergy

Project Lead, 6-person team · SCM 345, ASU · Fall 2025
Landed
Problem

Two merged companies had promised investors $35M in third-party savings. Their contract data, structures and price baselines didn't line up, so nobody could say whether the target was realistic.

Approach
  • Standardized the combined contract data and calculated each contract's remaining, forward-looking value
  • Sorted contracts into four quadrants by expiry timing and whether they allow termination for convenience
  • Assigned each contract a sourcing play: Combine Rate, Re-negotiate or Full RFP, with discounts set from internal best prices and external benchmarks
Output

A contract-level savings model and a phased plan: quick wins in the first 90 days, RFPs over 3–6 months, then rate resets as long-term contracts expire in 2028–2030.

$585Mtotal savings potential identified
$310M+realistically contractible by 2030

MIL

Askoll EVA: choosing a CRM platform and implementation partner

3-person team · Information Systems Management, Bocconi · Spring 2026
Landed
Problem

An Italian e-mobility maker expanding into France and Spain had outgrown its CRM. It had three bids, each a platform plus a partner: Microsoft Business Central, Salesforce and SAP CRM.

Approach
  • Modeled 5-year NPV of total cost of ownership and expected benefits for each bid
  • Built a weighted scorecard: strategic fit 33%, implementation feasibility 32%, business capability 20%, partner and governance fit 15%
  • Ran the same case through an AI research tool (NotebookLM) and compared its answer with ours
Output

Recommended Business Central. Salesforce returned slightly more per euro, but Business Central fit the company's existing Microsoft system and carried the lowest implementation risk. The AI tool reached the same pick but oversimplified the cost trade-off.

A six-module ERP for a student organization

Designer & owner · 2025–present · foundation of my Barrett honors thesis
In flight
Problem

Student clubs lose their memory every year when officers graduate. Budgets, vendors and event history lived in scattered spreadsheets and in people's heads.

Approach
  • Designed six modules in Google Sheets: events, membership, inventory, vendors, budget and marketing
  • Put every event through one approval pipeline: partner outreach → proposal → marketing review → budget review → approval → execution → feedback
  • Added inventory forecasts from consumption rates, attendance points with a member ranking, and a partner tracker
Output

The system the Student Entrepreneurship Organization runs on today. My IRB-approved honors thesis studies how a low-cost system like this can keep student organizations running through leadership turnover. Interviews are done; a survey comes next, with the defense in spring 2027.

THE FINE PRINT: a campus negotiation game

Game designer & facilitator · Student Entrepreneurship Organization · Fall 2026
In flight
Problem

My first simulation for the club, with six confidential executive roles and a scoring system, was too complex to teach and play in one evening.

Approach

I rebuilt it from scratch around one mechanic. Six two-person startup teams need three signed partners (money, distribution and delivery) to reach Demo Day. Twelve partners can each sign only two ventures, and every player carries a hidden clause.

The rule
If you are asked, you must answer honestly. If nobody asks, you owe nobody anything.
Output

A web app where each player opens a private role card with a 4-digit code. Facilitators get a master sheet with a conflict matrix, multiple endings and three debrief questions. A mid-game reveal shows which deals were built on questions nobody asked.

24players: 12 founders, 12 partners
6ventures racing for 3 signatures each
